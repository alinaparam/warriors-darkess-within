CHAPTER 7

🐾

Bristlefrost hurried to the top of the rise. Fresh scents came on the wind, and they made her excited. The wind did not smell of the lake, pine, or heather. Her heart filled with joy. This was the first time she had put a paw outside Clan territory. Rain had washed the forests and the moor for the last two days, but it stopped in time for the journey. And now, at last, the patrol had left the forest behind. Ahead, sunny valleys and grassy hills rolled to a blue horizon.

Her fur had been full of excitement ever since Squirrelflight told her that she would go on the patrol to find the Sisters with Rootspring. After the battle, she had thought that they would only see each other at Gatherings. She had accepted that she would live in ThunderClan while he stayed in SkyClan, even though they had admitted they had feelings for each other. But suddenly it did not matter so much that they could never be mates. They would be together for the next few days. That was enough. She would just have to keep her feelings in check. She could do that, surely? She was not a silly apprentice anymore. She was a warrior, and her Clan needed her to bring the Sisters back. She would focus on that. She felt almost sure the mission would be a success, because she and Rootspring worked so well together — almost as if they were Clanmates. Clanmates! The thought made happiness flow like warm honey through her body.

She looked over her shoulder at Rootspring, Spotfur, and Needleclaw, who walked behind. “It is beautiful!” she called.

The wind must have carried her mew away, because the others did not seem to hear her. Rootspring was keeping close to Spotfur. Spotfur's eyes barely lifted from the ground. Needleclaw looked around warily, as if a fox might be following them. Her heart sank. Were they not excited too?

Guilt pushed at Bristlefrost's belly. Was she the only cat who felt glad to be away from her Clan? In the days since Flipclaw and the others had left, ThunderClan had seemed lost. Every paw-length of the camp reminded Bristlefrost of the long moons when Ashfur had pretended to be Bramblestar and brought chaos to the Clan, turning Clanmates against one another. She knew that every warrior in ThunderClan felt ashamed of some things they had done. A fox-heart had tricked them, and they had fought with one another because of him. But she could not help feeling glad to be away from the forest for a while — and to be on a quest that might make everything right again.

She shook out her fur and hurried back to the patrol. “It looks like open country ahead,” she reported. “I cannot see any Twolegplaces; just a few dens here and there.”

Rootspring lifted his muzzle. Her words made him glad. “The Sisters must have gone this way,” he mewed. “We may find them by sunset.”

Needleclaw grunted. “That is not what you said last night,” she mewed. “Before we left, you said we could be looking for them until leaf-fall.”

Bristlefrost's fur moved crossly. “He was probably trying not to make you hope too much.” She swished her tail. “You know how careful Rootspring can be.”

Rootspring stared ahead. “Let us focus on finding them. Tree told us to look for a kittypet called Pancakes. He knows the Sisters and he can tell us if...” His mew stopped when they reached the top of the rise and he looked at the valleys and grassy hills. Had the view taken his breath away?

Bristlefrost watched him and waited.

“It is big,” he mewed quietly.

Spotfur stared across the hills. “I did not know there was so much land beyond Clan territory,” she breathed.

Needleclaw blinked. “Do you really think we are going to find the Sisters in all that?”

“We have to,” Bristlefrost told her. She was determined to hope. She remembered the huge she-cats who had come to the Clans when she was an apprentice. “They are our only chance to find Bramblestar's ghost again.”

Needleclaw swished her tail. “Where should we start?”

“Let us go down that way.” Bristlefrost nodded toward a valley full of ferns. “There is a Twoleg nest at the bottom. Pancakes might be there.”

Needleclaw stared at her sourly. “Rootspring is in charge,” she mewed. “He decides where we go. I know you are used to telling your Clanmates what to do, but on this patrol, Rootspring is the leader.”

Bristlefrost blinked at her. “We each have a voice,” she snapped. “There is no harm in any cat making a suggestion.” She tried to catch Rootspring's eye, hoping he would support her, but he was looking down the hill.

“Tree said Pancakes lives in a Twoleg nest in a valley beyond the Moonpool,” he mewed thoughtfully. “Bristlefrost might be right. That could be Pancakes's Twoleg nest.”

Triumph sparkled in Bristlefrost's fur. Needleclaw fluffed out her fur and walked away. “Thanks for supporting me,” she murmured as Spotfur went after the SkyClan warrior.

Rootspring's fur prickled. He felt shy. “It might take a while to find the Sisters.” He did not look at her. “We should all try to get along.”

Okay. Bristlefrost followed him while he walked after the others. But only because she is your littermate. Needleclaw could act like she had swallowed a wasp if she liked. Bristlefrost was determined to enjoy this mission. She lifted her eyes to the horizon. They were going to find the Sisters, no matter where they had wandered. For the first time since the battle, confidence pulsed in her paws. This was what she was meant to do. She was going to save ThunderClan and bring StarClan back.

Her paws ached by the time the sun went down behind the horizon. Dusk had spread deep blue shadows across the hillside while she followed Rootspring and Needleclaw along a ridge and went down into yet another valley. She had lost count of the hills they had climbed and the tiny Twoleg nests they had stopped at, looking for Pancakes. They had found three fat, pampered kittypets. One had been a queen, close to kitting. Another was an angry she-cat. The third was a tom, but he smelled different from the toms she knew, and he had the narrow forehead of a young paw. None of them had seen strange, big she-cats traveling in a group past their nests, and none had heard of a kittypet named Pancakes.

Perhaps we have come the wrong way. Doubt pulled at Bristlefrost's belly. She should have let Rootspring decide which way to take from the start. Why did I let him listen to me? He was walking beside Needleclaw now, as he had done for most of the day. She was disappointed that they had not had time to talk alone, but she thought it might be for the best. They had agreed they could never be mates, right? Perhaps it was easier not to get too close now.

“Mouse dung!” Spotfur's mew was full of pain.

Bristlefrost's heart beat faster. Was she hurt? She turned back to help as the other she-cat stopped and lifted her forepaw. “What happened?”

“It is okay.” Spotfur licked between her claws, then put them carefully onto the grass. “I did not see the dip.” She nodded toward a tiny hole where some animal must have dug.

Bristlefrost saw tiredness in Spotfur's eyes. “Rootspring!” she called.

He turned. His eyes went wide. “Are you two okay?”

“Spotfur hurt her paw,” Bristlefrost told him.

“It is not bad,” Spotfur mewed quickly.

“Can we make camp soon? She is getting tired,” Bristlefrost mewed. She did not listen to Spotfur's denial. “And my pads hurt.” She hated to talk about her own aching paws, but Spotfur needed rest. Who knew how many days of walking were ahead of them? There was no use making themselves tired at the start.

Rootspring looked around the valley. The grass ran down to a small Twoleg den among trees. A willow hung over the hillside a few tree-lengths from where they stood. Rootspring nodded toward it. “We could make camp there.”

Needleclaw was already walking toward the willow. She pushed her nose through the hanging leaves. “There is space between the roots to make nests,” she called from inside.

Rootspring followed her, and Bristlefrost slid after him, with Spotfur at her heels. Inside, branches covered a small stretch of grass, hidden like a cave behind a waterfall. A sandy hollow opened among the roots of the tree, safe enough to defend. It would be easy to build nests here.

“This looks like a good place to sleep,” Rootspring mewed.

“I can hunt,” Bristlefrost offered.

Rootspring shook his head. “I will hunt with Needleclaw.” He looked down at his paws shyly. “We have been hunting together since we were kits. We make a good team, kind of.”

Needleclaw moved her tail quickly. “We will catch enough for every cat,” she mewed. “You and Spotfur can build the nests.”

Bristlefrost made her fur stay smooth. We are not your apprentices.

“Or you could check that Twoleg den,” Rootspring suggested. “Pancakes might be there.”

“Okay.” Bristlefrost lowered her head. She had begun to lose hope of ever finding Pancakes. “But if we see prey, we will catch it,” she added. “Just in case you cannot find any.”

Needleclaw purred. “We will find some,” she mewed confidently, and pushed through the willow leaves.

“Good luck,” Rootspring called, following his littermate.

Bristlefrost ignored the jealousy that pulled at her belly. She went under the branches and headed downhill, toward the Twoleg nest.

Spotfur caught up to her. “Wait for me.”

Bristlefrost was relieved to see that her Clanmate was not limping. “Is your paw okay now?”

“I told you, it was nothing.”

“We should all be careful where we step,” Bristlefrost told her. “We cannot risk getting hurt. We do not have a medicine cat with us.”

“I guess.” Spotfur looked ahead.

“It is a shame we could not hunt,” Bristlefrost mewed. “I bet there is some great prey around here.” When Spotfur did not answer, she went on. “At least SkyClan cats like the same sort of prey we do. Imagine if Rootspring and Needleclaw were RiverClan cats. They would bring back fish instead of mice. Or ShadowClan cats!” She made a funny face. “I have heard ShadowClan warriors eat frogs.”

Bristlefrost looked at her Clanmate, hoping for an answer. But Spotfur did not move a whisker. Squirrelflight's words echoed in her mind. Spotfur needs something to take her mind off her grief. Perhaps she would feel better if she remembered why they had come. “If we find the Sisters, we might get Bramblestar back soon.” When Spotfur did not answer, Bristlefrost went on. “Would it not be great if we found Pancakes here? He could tell us where the Sisters have gone, and then—”

Spotfur stopped her. “Why do we not stop talking and focus on finding him.”

Bristlefrost's heart sank. Was there anything she could do to make her friend happy again? She walked on in silence. Maybe grief was something you could not rush.

A stone wall looked out from a stretch of ferns. A Twoleg boundary. She signaled to Spotfur with a quick flick of her tail and went low, moving like a snake through the long grass to the foot of the wall. Spotfur hurried behind her, belly touching the ground. They stopped, and Bristlefrost tasted the air. Cat-scent was almost hidden under the sweet scent of flowers, stronger than any she had smelled beside the lake. She nodded at Spotfur and jumped silently onto the wall. Fur touched stone as Spotfur jumped up beside her, and together they looked over the stretch of smooth grass that ran around the Twoleg nest. Something rustled in the shadows at one side. Pancakes?

Then she heard the grass swish farther up the slope, and her heart lurched. Were there more dogs here? She turned, and she saw two kits racing toward her. Their fluffy pelts were pale in the moonlight.

The kits stopped quickly in front of them and blinked with wide, excited eyes.

“Did you see Spike?” the larger kit asked.

“Did he scare you?” asked the other.

Both kits began to purr loudly, as if the idea was very funny.

Bristlefrost blinked at them. Why did these kits think dogs were funny? “He chased us!”

“He probably wanted to say hello,” the larger kit mewed.

“Spike loves cats,” the other kit told them. “We live with him.”

Bristlefrost flattened her ears, in horror. “Cats do not live with dogs.”

“Why not?” Both kits stared at her.

Bristlefrost stared back without answering. These kits were weird.

Spotfur shook out her fur. “Why does Spike smell like flowers?” she asked.

“Our Twolegs wash him,” the larger kit explained.

Bristlefrost shivered. Twolegs were even crueler to dogs than they were to cats. “Does a cat called Pancakes live around here?”

The kits looked at each other. “He used to,” the larger one mewed. “But he is gone now.”

“We are his kits,” the other told her. “I am Bacon.”

“I am Eggs.”

Bristlefrost tried to catch Spotfur's eye. Those are the most silly kittypet names ever. But Spotfur did not seem to find it funny. She looked like she would rather be curled up in her nest.

“Do you want to play tag?” Bacon asked eagerly.

“No thanks,” Spotfur told him. “We are looking for some friends.”

“We can be your friends,” Eggs told her.

“We are looking for old friends,” Spotfur mewed wearily. “Not new ones.”

Eggs looked very sad.

Bristlefrost shot Spotfur a warning glance. These kits might know something useful. “I am sure you would make great friends,” she added. “But we cannot stay, I am afraid. We are on a mission—”

Spotfur's tail flicked impatiently. “Have you ever seen any big, furry she-cats?”

The kits looked at each other thoughtfully.

“No,” Eggs mewed. “I do not think so.”

Spotfur leaned closer. “What about toms?”

Bristlefrost watched the kits frown. They were clearly trying to remember. She tried prompting them. “They might have had funny names like Stone or Branch or—”

Bacon's ears pricked. “Leaf!”

Bristlefrost's heart leaped. Leaf sounded like the sort of name the Sisters would give to a kit.

Eggs' fluffy tail shot up with excitement. “We know a tom called Leaf,” he mewed. “He stopped here and played tag with us once.”

“Are you sure you do not want to play tag?” Bacon asked. “It is really fun.”

“No thanks,” Bristlefrost told him.

Spotfur's fur was rippling along her spine. “Did this Leaf say where he came from or where he was going?”

“He said he had left a group of cats that was heading toward the river to make their camp for the warm season,” Eggs replied.

“Which river?” Spotfur asked.

“He said it was that way.” Eggs nodded toward the hillside covered with ferns.

Bristlefrost could hardly keep her paws still. She could not wait to get back to the willow and tell Rootspring. They had been heading the right way after all. All they had to do now was find the river and follow it.

She just hoped they could persuade the Sisters to return to the lake. For the first time, a chilling thought flashed through her mind. What if the Sisters refused to come?

“Of course they will come,” Needleclaw mewed when Bristlefrost shared her fear. The black-and-white she-cat leaned back contentedly, pushing away the remnants of the mouse she had been eating.

“Why should they?” Bristlefrost had hardly touched any of the prey Rootspring and Needleclaw had brought back. The idea that the Sisters might refuse to help made her belly tight with worry.

“We will persuade them.” Needleclaw half closed her eyes.

With hope in her chest, Bristlefrost jumped down and landed on the soft lawn. She stood up straight. “Try to look friendly,” she whispered to Spotfur when she landed beside her.

“I know.” Spotfur shot her a look. “This is the fourth time we have done this.”

Bristlefrost's fur prickled. She felt shy again. “I am just trying to be careful.” She led the way across the grass. She kept her fur smooth and her ears up, as if she were greeting an old friend. “Hey,” she mewed when they came near the nest. The moon had risen behind the hills. She narrowed her eyes against its bright light while she looked into the shadows. Something moved there. She nodded for Spotfur to stop and spoke again. “We are just passing through,” she mewed in a light voice. “We are looking for some friends.”

She waited. Her heart beat faster when paws brushed the earth. She hoped it was a friendly kittypet. She was too tired for a fight. Bright, round eyes glittered from the darkness. Then a new scent touched her nose. Through the sweet smell of flowers, she smelled dog.

Her fur stood up when a low growl sounded in the darkness. The dog must have hidden its smell by rolling in petals. Now she could see its outline: a stocky dog, shaking with excitement.

The air seemed to split when the dog let out a howl. Bristlefrost pressed against Spotfur. “Run!” She felt Spotfur turn. The smell of fear came from her fur. Bristlefrost followed her, running across the grass so fast that it blurred under her paws.

Loud barking exploded behind them. Heavy paws beat on the ground. Hot breath touched her tail, and the bad smell of crow-food almost made her gag. Spotfur ran up the wall ahead of her. She stopped only long enough to look back at Bristlefrost, then jumped down the other side. Bristlefrost jumped after her, hoping with all her heart that the dog could not follow.

She followed Spotfur up the slope. She was glad that Spotfur was going away from the willow. If the dog found their trail, it might follow it back to their camp. She let out a shaking breath when she understood that the dog's bark had not followed them over the wall. Then a Twoleg cried out. She turned and saw a figure in the moonlight. It moved toward the dog, caught it, and pulled it inside the nest.

Bristlefrost stood and breathed fast, and her fur slowly lay down again. Spotfur walked back to her side. The gray-and-white she-cat was breathing heavily, and her eyes shone with fear. They stared at the Twoleg nest.

Rootspring tore a strip of flesh from a thrush and laid it on the ground in front of Bristlefrost. “Needleclaw always gets her way in the end,” he murmured wryly. “If any cat can persuade the Sisters to come back, she can.” He pushed the strip closer. “You persuaded warriors from all different Clans to fight Ashfur,” he reminded her. “Between the two of you, you will make them see that it is the right thing to do.”

Bristlefrost blinked at him. The moon had risen high above the willow. Its light fell like water between the leaves. Spotfur had said she was too tired to eat, and she was already sleeping in the fern nest Bristlefrost had made for her. A mouse lay beside it for when she woke up. “But they have no reason to help Bramblestar,” she mewed. “Or us. After all, he was not kind to them when they last tried to settle near the lake, even though that was only for a short time. If they are still upset—”

“Eat.” Rootspring pushed the strip of flesh again. “You will need your strength tomorrow. We do not know how far away this river is.”

“Or how long ago the Sisters passed through,” Needleclaw added.

“But we will find them.” Rootspring looked at them both, and his eyes gleamed with determination. “And we will persuade them to come back with us.”

Bristlefrost wanted to believe him. She was touched that he was trying so hard to make her sure it would be okay. She looked into his eyes. Did he believe it? His eyes shone. He seemed really excited. She took a bite of the flesh. Her belly growled, because it suddenly remembered how hungry it was. She took another bite and swallowed it eagerly.

Rootspring purred and pulled the rest of the thrush closer. He had been right about hunting well with Needleclaw. They had brought back enough prey to feed a whole warriors' den. There would be plenty left for the morning.

Needleclaw stretched. “I am sleepy.”

“Some cat needs to guard the camp,” Rootspring mewed.

Bristlefrost swallowed her mouthful. “I can take the first shift.”

“Good.” Needleclaw yawned.

Bristlefrost looked at Spotfur. Should she take Spotfur's shift too? The gray-and-white she-cat had seemed very tired.

“I will sit with you,” Rootspring offered. “I am not really tired.”

“Should you not sleep while you can?” Needleclaw stood up and walked to her nest, pulling at the ferns to make them softer.

Rootspring did not answer. Instead he told her, “I will wake you when it is your turn.”

She looked at him but did not argue. She curled up in the nest and closed her eyes.

When Bristlefrost finished the thrush, she heard the black-and-white she-cat breathing in deep sleep. She swallowed the last piece and licked her lips. “There is no need for you to stay up,” she told Rootspring.

“I want to.” He stood up and walked out through the hanging willow branches.

Bristlefrost followed him. Her heart moved fast like a bird in her chest. She felt suddenly awkward. What would they talk about? Perhaps they should not talk at all. They had agreed to be friends and nothing more. But it was hard to be just friends, alone in the moonlight. Especially when being near Rootspring made her fur tingle like this. Perhaps she should wake Spotfur and ask her to change watches.

Rootspring sat on the grass outside the willow den and looked along the valley. Around him, ferns moved in the light wind, swishing like the lake moves small stones against the shore. Bristlefrost sat down stiffly beside him. She had wanted to be alone with him, and now she was. But this was not how she had imagined it. She had thought it would be wonderful. And yet he suddenly seemed like a stranger. His fur shone. His head had grown broad. And his shoulders too. She stopped a shiver and tried to remember him as the tall, thin apprentice she had rescued from the lake. Or the awkward young apprentice who had once crossed the ThunderClan border to bring her prey.

He fluffed out his fur against the cold, and his fur touched hers. She tried not to jump when her own fur seemed to spark where they touched. She stared ahead, not daring to look at him.

“It is a relief to be away from the Clans for a while, isn't it?” Rootspring mewed.

She felt him looking at her and wrapped her tail tighter around her paws. She could not stop thinking about the Clan she had left. It was now missing several warriors. “I guess...”

“What is wrong?”

She could feel his eyes like the heat of the sun on her fur. Slowly, she turned to meet them.

“Some of our Clanmates have left.” His eyes widened, but he said nothing. “With everything that has been going on, not every warrior in ThunderClan thinks it is the right place for them anymore. They have... 'gone for a wander,' whatever that means...”

She felt his tail rest gently on her back. The chill of the night ran away like a trespasser on a Clan's border. “Oh no...” He moved so that his side pressed right against her side. “Do you think they are going to come back?”

“I do not know,” she admitted. “I hope so, but... I do not know. Even Graystripe left. Squirrelflight took that hardest of all.”

Rootspring sat with her in silence for a moment, until he finally murmured, “That must be very worrying.”

She nodded. “It is, but... I am glad to be here with you.”

His eyes shone with warmth, unchanged from the first time they had met. “The moon seems bigger out here,” he mewed softly.

“Yes.” Bristlefrost's shoulders loosened. “Yes, it does.” Sitting beside Rootspring, with their fur touching, suddenly seemed as natural as sleeping. I was right, she thought happily. This is wonderful.

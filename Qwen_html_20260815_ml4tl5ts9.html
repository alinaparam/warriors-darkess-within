Leafstar's den: the air was hot and heavy. In the night, thick clouds came into the sky. They kept the heat of the last days, and the air pressed down on everything. Rootspring's fur itched. He wanted to talk with Leafstar outside, but the air outside was not much fresher. And Tree wanted them to talk in private. He did not want to make their Clanmates afraid.

Leafstar looked at Rootspring and waited for his words. Her yellow eyes shone in the dark den. Rootspring looked at Tree. He hoped that Tree would speak, but Tree only looked back at him. He waited too, just like Leafstar.

Rootspring lifted his head. He remembered: he was a warrior now, and he could speak for himself. “We have not seen any ghosts,” he began.

“After the battle...” Rootspring was sorry he did not plan his words. “So many cats died. We thought that their ghosts would come to the vigil.” He looked into Leafstar's eyes. She looked at him, and her eyes were dark with worry. Does she think I am strange because I want to see ghosts?

“They went to StarClan, didn't they?” Leafstar did not understand.

“But I saw ghosts after the first attack on Bramblestar,” Rootspring explained. “When Conefoot and Stemleaf died, I saw their ghosts too. And I saw Bramblestar...”

“Recently?” Leafstar moved closer.

“No.” Rootspring looked down. I am explaining this very badly. “I have not seen Bramblestar for a whole moon.”

Leafstar's shoulders went down. She was sad. Rootspring took a breath and tried again.

“After Ashfur took Bramblestar's body, I saw ghosts,” he mewed. “Ghosts like Bramblestar and Stemleaf. They were still in the forest. But this time, the dead warriors are not around.”

“But that is a good thing, isn't it?” Leafstar still did not understand. “It means they found their way to StarClan.”

Rootspring looked at her. She still did not understand the main thing.

Tree lowered his head politely. “I do not think that is possible now.”

“But dead warriors always go to StarClan. That is how it always is.” Leafstar looked at him.

“But for a long time we could not reach StarClan,” Tree went on. “Maybe there is no place for them to go.” He stopped for a moment. Leafstar closed her eyes.

At last she seemed to understand. “You think they cannot reach StarClan either.”

“I do not know,” Tree mewed. “But it seems true, doesn't it?” He did not wait for an answer. “And if it is true, the dead must still be here in the forest, where we can see them.” He looked at Rootspring.

“That is why we thought we would see them at the vigil,” Rootspring mewed. “But none of them came.”

“Maybe they just did not want to watch their own vigil.” Leafstar wanted to hope.

Rootspring felt sorry for his leader. He did not want to make her sad, but she had to know the truth. “On our way back from the island, we saw a kittypet ghost,” he told her. “She said she saw some warrior ghosts, but they kept fading away.” He looked straight into her eyes. “She said they looked scared.”

Tree came closer. “We think they are disappearing.”

“Into nothing,” Rootspring finished his father's thought. His voice was quiet and sad.

Leafstar looked at them for a moment, then sighed. “You must tell the other Clans,” she mewed. “They must know what is happening to their dead Clanmates.”

Rootspring's heart fell. It was already hard to tell her.

“I will send word to the Clan leaders. They must meet me today,” Leafstar went on. “This evening we will go to the island. You and Tree can tell them what you told me.”

Rootspring lowered his head to Leafstar. “Okay,” he mewed. He looked at Tree. His father would be with him, and that made him glad.

“...So we think they may be fading into nothing.” Rootspring looked at the leaders with fear. The leaders brought their medicine cats. The light over the lake grew dim, and the cats listened with their ears up. Water touched the shore a few tail-lengths away. The cats stood at the far end of the island, not far from the clearing. Maybe they did not want to break the silence of yesterday's vigil.

A cool wind blew away the heavy heat. A storm with rain was coming. It was already visible as a dark shadow over the far moor. Rootspring fluffed out his fur and waited for someone to speak.

Tigerstar moved his paws. The ShadowClan leader looked strangely calm, as if the news answered something heavy in his mind. “If it is true, and the dead are disappearing, we must accept that Bramblestar's ghost is already gone.”

Squirrelflight looked at him and could not believe it. “How can you say such a thing?”

The fur on Harestar's back stood up. “If our dead Clanmates are disappearing,” the WindClan leader growled, “we must help them.”

“How?” Leafstar asked the medicine cats.

Willowshine blinked at her. She did not seem to hear; she was still trying to accept the news. “StarClan would not leave the dead, even if they left us!”

Kestrelflight's eyes were wide. “How can we help them without StarClan?”

“We must try harder to talk to them,” Frecklewish mewed.

Jayfeather looked ahead with his blind eyes. “We tried everything we know.”

Tigerstar moved his tail from side to side. “Ashfur is the one who caused this. If we stop him, it may fix everything.”

Rootspring's heart beat faster. There was hard determination in the ShadowClan leader's voice. The other leaders looked at each other with fear.

“You wanted to hurt my mate before we knew about this,” Squirrelflight growled. “Do not try to make any cat believe that this is about the Clans.”

Tigerstar looked straight at her. “We are keeping the cat who hurt Bramblestar alive for no reason.”

“He has Bramblestar's body!” Squirrelflight said angrily.

“It sounds like Bramblestar does not need it anymore,” Tigerstar growled darkly.

“We do not know that.” Leafstar walked between the two warriors. The fur on her back stood up.

Rootspring stopped breathing for a moment. Tigerstar's eyes turned to him.

“You said it was a moon since you last saw Bramblestar, right?” the ShadowClan leader asked.

Rootspring hesitated. He saw great pain in Squirrelflight's eyes. “Yes,” he mewed quietly.

Tigerstar's tail moved quickly. “See?” He looked at the other leaders. “If cats who died a quarter moon ago are already fading, Bramblestar must be gone. Why are we keeping Ashfur alive? Maybe he is blocking our way to StarClan. We should stop him forever and finish it. Then everything can go back to normal.”

Harestar frowned. “Are you sure it is so simple?”

“Of course I am not sure,” Tigerstar answered angrily. “But you heard Ashfur's threat. He promised to make trouble for the living and the dead. We must stop him!”

Mistystar moved her head to one side and thought. “Tigerstar is right in a way. If we are too late to save Bramblestar, we may still stop our dead Clanmates from disappearing.”

“But Rootspring was not the last cat to see Bramblestar.” Squirrelflight looked very upset. Her front paws pressed into the ground. “Shadowsight saw him in the Dark Forest. His ghost must still be somewhere.” She looked at Jayfeather with despair. When he did not speak, she looked at the other medicine cats. Then her body went stiff. “Where is Shadowsight?” Did she only now see that ShadowClan's medicine cat was not there?

Tigerstar moved his paws. “Shadowsight decided to focus on training and make his skills better for now.”

Mothwing lowered her head. “He felt he went a little too fast, and he needed to go back to the basics for a time.”

Squirrelflight pressed her ears down. “Is he being punished for bringing messages from Ashfur?”

“Of course not,” Tigerstar mewed. Rootspring narrowed his eyes. The ShadowClan leader answered a little too quickly.

Squirrelflight did not believe it either. “If any cat should be angry at Shadowsight, it is me,” she mewed. “But I am not. He thought he was doing the right thing. He is young. How could he know better?”

“That is right,” Mothwing mewed. “He is a little too young to be a full medicine cat. Some more training will not hurt him.”

Rootspring's heart hurt for Shadowsight. They became close over the last few moons. How bad to be made an apprentice again! All this trouble was not Shadowsight's fault; Ashfur caused it. The dark warrior tricked everyone.

Squirrelflight's tail moved angrily. “It does not matter what you decided about Shadowsight's training. The fact is: he saw Bramblestar in the Dark Forest, so his ghost is still out there. We cannot hurt Ashfur before we give Bramblestar a chance to take his body back.”

Tigerstar grunted. “So how long do you want us to wait? One moon? Two? How long is enough?”

“How long?” Squirrelflight looked at the ShadowClan leader.

Next to Rootspring, Tree moved uneasily. Harestar looked at the ground. An awkward silence fell over the cats. It was not fair to ask Squirrelflight when she would be ready to lose a cat she loved. But the other leaders had a point too. Ashfur threatened the living and the dead. And now it seemed that their Clanmates were fading into nothing. They had to do something before the dead warriors disappeared forever.

Squirrelflight turned to Leafstar. “Do you think we should stop Ashfur forever while there is a chance that Bramblestar will return?”

Leafstar did not look at the ThunderClan warrior. “There may be no time to wait, if we want to stop our Clanmates from disappearing.”

Tigerstar looked at Squirrelflight and begged with his eyes. “What else can we do? We can only stop the one cat who certainly caused all this trouble.”

Alderheart stepped forward and looked at the cats. His tail moved nervously. “We do not even know if Ashfur will stop when Bramblestar's body dies.”

“What do you mean?” Mistystar lifted her ears.

“Can we be sure Ashfur really needs Bramblestar's body?” Alderheart asked. “Shadowsight said he saw Ashfur's spirit leave it. What if, when the body dies, Ashfur just goes from one body into another?”

Rootspring looked at the ThunderClan medicine cat. Did that mean nothing could stop Ashfur? Fear went through his fur when Alderheart went on.

“If Ashfur cannot die, then Bramblestar would die for no good reason.”

Tigerstar was suddenly very still.

Harestar blinked. “Maybe when Bramblestar's body dies, Bramblestar can come back into it,” he mewed with hope.

Mistystar moved closer. “You found your way back to your body after you died in the battle. Why can Bramblestar not do the same?”

Harestar nodded quickly. “Maybe he is waiting for his body to die, so he can return to it.”

“And if he is not?” Squirrelflight answered angrily. “If he cannot return to his body? What then?”

Tigerstar's eyes grew dark. “Then we will know he is truly gone.”

Squirrelflight moved back in fear, as if the ShadowClan leader had hit out with his sharp claws.

Rootspring's thoughts went round and round. Would Tigerstar really hurt a Clan leader? What if he destroyed Bramblestar's body, and then they found that Ashfur's spirit went on making trouble for the Clans? The ShadowClan leader would not be so careless, would he?...

Leafstar lifted her head. “Before we do anything, we must find out if Bramblestar is really gone.”

Squirrelflight seemed not to listen. Her fur moved a little, as if she was trying not to shake. Jayfeather came closer and pressed against her. Leafstar looked at the other leaders with a question.

“Are we sure we have waited long enough for him to return?” the SkyClan leader asked.

“He had a lot of time to show himself,” Tigerstar growled. “We have more medicine cats than before.” His eyes turned quickly to Tree and Rootspring. “And those two.” Rootspring felt only a little embarrassed at the ShadowClan leader's tone. At least no other cats had shown that Tigerstar did not respect the power that he and his father shared. “Surely one of them would have seen Bramblestar, if he is still around.”

Mistystar looked worried. “Maybe he really is gone.”

Squirrelflight was shaking now and did not hide it. Her eyes were empty with fear. “You all think we should hurt Bramblestar?”

“We have to do something,” Tigerstar mewed softly.

“And what if you are wrong?” She looked at him angrily. “You do not know! You admitted you were not sure. And still you would hurt a warrior, just because it might fix things you do not even fully understand!”

Rootspring's heart pressed in his throat. “She is right.” He was the reason this meeting happened. He could not let the Clan leaders make a decision that could cost Bramblestar everything. He had to make sure they truly understood how important it was. He went on, hoping his mew did not shake. “I was the first cat to see Bramblestar's ghost. I did not want to see him at first, but he kept following me. He would not give up until I admitted I could see him. He will not have given up now. And I cannot give up on him, not after he fought so hard to make me see him. I wish I understood more. I wish I knew what was happening. But I am not an expert...”

When his words ended, Rootspring looked at the leaders and begged with his eyes. “Please, give him more time.”

Tree walked forward. “Rootspring is right. We need to be sure we know what we are doing.” Rootspring blinked gratefully at his father. “There are cats who know more about wandering spirits than we do,” Tree went on. “You know who I mean.”

Rootspring's heart jumped because he understood. “The Sisters?” The group of strange she-cats — his kin and Tree's — were born with the power to see ghosts. They accepted it as part of their everyday life.

Tigerstar rolled his eyes. “That group of troublemakers.”

Tree ignored him. “I do not know for sure that they can help, but they might at least understand what is happening.” His eyes moved around to the medicine cats. “You know StarClan. But the Sisters know about the dead who stay with us to walk the forests. If any cat can find out if Bramblestar is truly gone, it will be them.” He looked into the ShadowClan leader's cold eyes. “We need advice,” he mewed. “And they are the best cats to give it.”

Leafstar frowned. “But we tried one of their ceremonies before the battle,” she reminded them. “It did not work. Why will it work this time?”

“We are not trying to bring him back now,” Tree told her. “We are trying to find out if he is still beside the lake at all.”

Rootspring nodded. He tried not to look at Squirrelflight. Her jaw was tight, as if claws had scratched her. “Besides, copying a Sisters ceremony is not the same as asking them to do it themselves,” he mewed. “They know how it works.”

Tree nodded. “And they are more powerful.”

Kestrelflight snorted. “Rogues are not more powerful than StarClan.”

“The Sisters are not rogues!” Squirrelflight mewed hotly.

Tree's calm eyes stayed on Kestrelflight. “StarClan is not with us right now,” he reminded him. “We need all the help we can get.”

Squirrelflight's tail was moving nervously. “Tree's idea is a good one,” she mewed. “I spent time with the Sisters. I trust them. And I will respect what they say about Bramblestar” — she looked at Tigerstar with anger — “even if they say he is gone.”

Harestar looked unsure. “Why should we bring outsiders to solve our problems?”

“What if their help makes things worse?” Mistystar agreed.

Leafstar looked at her. “Can it get worse than a dead warrior taking a leader's body to harm the Clans?”

When no cat answered, Rootspring fluffed out his fur. The wind was getting cold. Rain had reached the far shore and was making little circles on the surface of the lake. The leaders did not seem to notice. They stood still, and their fur was smooth.

“Okay.” Leafstar nodded firmly. “Let us ask the Sisters. We are dealing with things we do not understand. And we would never forgive ourselves if we made the wrong decision. We must take a little more time and get advice from cats who are used to dealing with ghosts.”

Tigerstar huffed softly to himself but did not object. Harestar and Mistystar looked at each other. When they did not speak, Leafstar went on.

“Tree.” She blinked at the yellow tom. “Do you know where we can find the Sisters now?”

“It is hard to know for sure,” he told her. “But greenleaf is nearly here, so they will be looking for land away from Twolegs. That means they will probably go across the hills.” He looked past the forest, where the trees met the moors and the Moonpool lay beyond.

Squirrelflight moved closer. “How far will they have gone?” she mewed.

“I cannot be sure,” Tree told her. “But the Sisters are hard to miss. Some cat will have seen them pass.”

Mistystar's fur moved a little. “The sooner we find them, the better,” she mewed quickly.

Tigerstar growled. “More delays.”

“It is better to wait than to make a mistake we cannot take back,” Harestar told him. “Let us find these Sisters, see what they have to say, and then stop Ashfur once and for all.”

Rootspring hoped it was that simple. He wondered if his father would find the Sisters as easily as he hoped.

Mothwing looked worried. “And if the Sisters cannot find Bramblestar either?”

“Then we will have no choice,” Tigerstar mewed. Behind him, the lake was disappearing behind a thin gray mist. “We will have to stop Ashfur forever. It may be our only hope.”

Squirrelflight curled her claws into the ground, as if to hold herself steady.

Harestar lowered his head in agreement. “With any luck, it will mean Bramblestar can begin a new life as himself, just as I did during the battle.”

“Okay,” Leafstar nodded. “We will find the Sisters and get their help.”

“They are not coming onto ShadowClan land,” Tigerstar growled.

Harestar puffed out his chest. “Not WindClan either.”

Leafstar looked tired. “Very well. They may only set paw on SkyClan land.”

“And talk to SkyClan cats,” Mistystar added.

Leafstar waited while the other leaders murmured in agreement. Then she nodded to Tree. “When can you leave?”

The yellow tom looked into her eyes. “I am not going,” he told her. “I owe the Sisters nothing and they owe me nothing, and that is the way I like it.”

Rootspring looked at his father. Rain began to beat on the ground around the cats. “But you have to go! You are their kin. You can make them help us better than any other cat.”

Tree blinked at him. “You are their kin too. And your relationship with them is much less complicated than mine.”

“You want me to go?” Rootspring shook raindrops from his eyes. “I have not seen them since I was a kit! I have never left Clan territory before. Do you think I can do it?”

“Of course you can.” Tree looked at him warmly. “There is no cat I would trust more with this mission.”

He really believes in me. Rootspring felt the earth pressing up against his paws. It felt deep and strong. I think I can do this too. “Okay.” He turned to Leafstar. “I will find the Sisters.”

Her eyes shone. “Thank you, Rootspring.” Pride filled his chest as she went on. “Take Needleclaw with you.” She shook raindrops from her whiskers. “She is the Sisters' kin too. I hope they will welcome you both.” The SkyClan leader turned her muzzle to the other leaders. “Does any other Clan want to send cats on this mission?”

Tigerstar and Harestar did not look at her. Mistystar did not speak, but Squirrelflight stood up straight.

“Bristlefrost will go with them,” the ThunderClan leader mewed. “And Spotfur.”

Rootspring blinked at the ThunderClan leader. She sounded very sure. “Is Spotfur not still mourning Stemleaf?”

“A mission will remind her that she is a warrior and her Clan needs her,” Squirrelflight told him. “Bristlefrost will take care of her.”

Butterflies seemed to fly like leaves in Rootspring's belly. He would spend time with Bristlefrost again. He tried to push away his excitement, hoping the others could not feel it. Would it be hard to be so close to her? No. They were both warriors now, and they were being trusted with an important mission. Neither of them would let their feelings get in the way of that. Besides, they would not be alone; Spotfur and Needleclaw would be there too.

We are friends. He and Bristlefrost had already decided that was all they could be. He stood in the rain while the others began to walk to the cover of the trees. I just hope I am strong enough to stay true to that decision.

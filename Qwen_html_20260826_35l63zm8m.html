CHAPTER 8

Shadowsight stretched and climbed out of his nest. Morning sunshine came through the entrance of the medicine den while he went to the herb store. Mothwing and Puddleshine had already left. Were they gathering herbs? Or perhaps one of their Clanmates had needed their help. Frustration pushed at Shadowsight's belly. They used to share things like that with him. But now he had lost his full medicine-cat status, and they murmured to each other as if they were afraid he would hear. Now he never knew where his denmates had gone or which Clanmate was sick.

At least they still let him treat one cat. Shadowsight went to the herb store. He wondered which herbs the prisoner would need today. Not poppy seed or marigold. Ashfur's battle wounds had closed well and were not inflamed anymore. He could take some goldenrod and dock to make the healing faster. He crouched beside the store and put his paw inside.

“What are you doing?” Mothwing's sharp mew made him jump around. She stood at the den entrance and frowned at him.

“I was getting herbs for Ashfur.” Shadowsight's fur grew hot with embarrassment, as if he had just been caught stealing from the pile of fresh prey.

“I left you a bundle.” Mothwing nodded toward a leaf wrap at the edge of the den.

“Is there any goldenrod in it?” Shadowsight asked. “Or dock?”

“There is everything you need.” Mothwing took the bundle in her jaws and carried it across the den. She dropped it at his paws. “You had better stay out of the herb store for now.”

He blinked at her as if she had scratched his muzzle. Puddleshine had encouraged him to study the herb store so that he knew where everything was kept. “But I am still a medicine-cat apprentice,” he reminded her.

“You are under supervision,” she mewed sourly. “Stay away from the herb store unless Puddleshine or I tell you otherwise.” She slid past him and looked into the store, as if checking for any damage he might have done.

Angrily, Shadowsight took the herb bundle and walked out of the den.

He passed Scorchfur and Snowbird. They were sharing a mouse beside the long grass at the edge of the clearing. Blazefire and Berryheart were play-fighting nearby, and Gullswoop and Hollowspring dragged old nests out of the warriors' den. Shadowsight's fur twitched. He felt shy. Were they staring at him? He kept his eyes on the hollow tree. The whole Clan must know by now that he had lost his medicine-cat status. It made him feel as if he were walking around camp without his fur.

Breezepelt and Sedgewhisker were guarding the bramble enclosure this morning. The two WindClan warriors looked small and sleek beside the ShadowClan cats.

Breezepelt stretched deeply, as if sitting had made him stiff. “I am looking forward to getting back to the moor and feeling the wind in my fur again.”

“And sunshine,” Sedgewhisker mewed sadly. “This forest makes me feel like I am living underground.” She looked at the strips of blue sky between the tops of the pines. “I am surprised ShadowClan cats do not have moss growing between their claws.”

Breezepelt purred with amusement, then stopped when he noticed Shadowsight walking toward him. He sat up stiffly. Sedgewhisker sat up straight and stared ahead.

Shadowsight's paws pricked. Had they stopped talking because a ShadowClan cat was coming closer? Or was it because I am coming? He felt a chill run through his fur as he came near. He had been so focused on the possibility that every cat in his Clan knew about his change in status, he had not thought about how far the news might already have spread into the other Clans. Does every cat know? He nodded a greeting as he passed. “Hey.”

“Hi.” Breezepelt's reply was barely a mumble.

Sedgewhisker grunted, avoiding Shadowsight's eye.

Shadowsight walked past them, shrinking under his fur. He felt sure now: All the Clans know I am a failed medicine cat, and they blame me for Ashfur coming to ThunderClan. When bad thoughts filled his head, Ashfur squeezed out of his den in the hollow tree.

The dark warrior blinked at Shadowsight as if he, at least, was glad to see him. “Hi, Shadowsight.” He waited for Shadowsight to put down his bundle, then sat down in a patch of sunshine near the enclosure wall. “What have you got for me today?”

I do not know yet. Shadowsight unrolled the leaf. He was irritated again when he saw a few damp nettle leaves inside. These drooping leaves would not help much. He would go through the motions anyway and make an ointment for Ashfur's worst scratches. Bitterly, he began to chew the leaves.

Ashfur lay down on his belly and waited. He was used to the daily routine now. “I need some exercise,” he mewed absently.

Shadowsight spat nettle pulp onto the unrolled leaf and put some onto Ashfur's paw. “It is best for you to rest until your wounds are completely healed.”

“I need to stay fit,” Ashfur complained.

“Why?” Shadowsight parted the fur on Ashfur's side with a paw and began to spread the pulp onto the long scab that ran along it.

“The Clans cannot keep me trapped here forever,” Ashfur mewed.

Just until they find a way to help Bramblestar get his body back, Shadowsight thought. He still found it strange to treat a warrior who looked like one cat but talked like another.

Ashfur spoke again. “Have you heard any news from ThunderClan?”

Shadowsight went stiff. Why does he want to know? “No,” he answered. The leaders and medicine cats had met on the island three days ago, but he had not been allowed to attend. He swallowed back his anger.

“What about Squirrelflight?” Ashfur's eyes narrowed with interest.

“No cat has mentioned her.” Shadowsight felt wary. What was the dark warrior planning?

“Do you think she will visit me again?”

Did he want a chance to take revenge on the ThunderClan leader for tricking him? Shadowsight put his paw into the pulp once more and began to smear it over a bite mark at the back of Ashfur's neck. “I am surprised you want to see her again,” he mewed casually.

“She could not help what happened last time.” Ashfur shook his head. “The other leaders must have forced her to betray me like that.” He moved so that Shadowsight could reach a scar on his hind leg. “They were listening to every word she said. If she had not betrayed me, they would have made her a prisoner too.” His eyes sparked, as if he enjoyed the thought of having Squirrelflight as his denmate in the hollow tree.

Shadowsight sat back on his haunches. Did Ashfur really believe she had not wanted to betray him? “I do not think Squirrelflight will visit you again.”

“If she could, we could talk things out.” Ashfur's eyes moved thoughtfully. “We might be able to come to an agreement.”

Shadowsight's eyes widened. Was Ashfur crazy? “Like what?”

“If she agreed to leave with me, I would not bother the Clans anymore.” His eyes suddenly fixed on Shadowsight. Alarm sparked in Shadowsight's belly when he saw determination in them. “Then no cat would have to suffer.” There was menace in the dark warrior's voice.

Shadowsight turned away and put more pulp onto his paw. He walked around Ashfur and spread it along the scar on his tail. “What if Squirrelflight does not want to leave with you?” he asked carefully.

“She must.” A growl rumbled in Ashfur's throat. “She is a warrior. She will do anything for her Clan.”

Shadowsight felt sick. Ashfur would clearly be ready to take Squirrelflight with him against her will. Did he hope that threatening the Clans would make her agree to go? Would she give up her own life like that? Shadowsight's heart lurched. She might. He rubbed the last of the pulp over Ashfur's torn ear tips. Squirrelflight must never find out. The Clans must not let her destroy her life to save them. There had to be another way. He rolled the empty leaf back up. “I am done,” he told Ashfur.

Ashfur stood up and looked at the wounds Shadowsight had treated. “You are still a good medicine cat,” he mewed. “But what is the point of being good at healing if you are not allowed to heal anymore?”

“I am healing you.” Shadowsight met his eyes, refusing to be provoked.

“And when I am gone?” Ashfur went on. “What will you do then? Are you going to spend the rest of your life cleaning other cats' bedding?”

“Of course not.” Doubt moved in Shadowsight's belly. Tigerstar and Puddleshine would let him go back to real medicine-cat work eventually, right? “As soon as StarClan returns—”

Ashfur's whiskers moved with amusement. “StarClan is not coming back.”

“They will.” They have to.

“Do you really think your Clanmates will believe any message you pass on from StarClan ever again?” Ashfur's eyes burned into Shadowsight's.

Shadowsight swallowed back panic. Will they?

Ashfur moved closer. “And you cannot become a warrior,” he murmured. “You are not trained. You have no skills.”

“I could learn!” Shadowsight puffed out his chest.

“I suppose you could be a mediator.” Ashfur sneered. “Or whatever it is Tree calls himself so that he can stay in the Clans without becoming a real warrior.”

Shadowsight stared at the impostor. “Why do you enjoy making other cats miserable?”

“We are more alike than you think,” Ashfur murmured.

“No, we are not!” Shadowsight lashed his tail.

Ashfur tipped his head. Shadowsight's anger clearly did not impress him. “I am a warrior who is not a warrior, and you are a medicine cat who is not a medicine cat.”

“You mean you were a warrior. You have been dead a long time!” Shadowsight took the empty leaf and marched out of the enclosure.

“I do not feel dead, thanks to this body!” Ashfur called after him.

Shadowsight ignored him and walked past Breezepelt and Sedgewhisker. They were still standing guard outside. Breezepelt blinked at him in surprise. The WindClan warrior was staring at him as if he had two tails.

Shadowsight spat out the leaf. “What?” he demanded angrily.

Breezepelt looked curious. “Are you really allowed to treat him alone?” He nodded toward Ashfur. Ashfur had walked to the back of the enclosure and lay down in the shade.

“Of course I am!”

Breezepelt glanced at his Clanmate uncertainly.

Shadowsight turned around fast and glared at Sedgewhisker. “Do you have a problem with that?”

“We just thought you were not a medicine cat anymore,” Sedgewhisker mewed.

So the rest of the Clans did know that Tigerstar had taken his status away. Fury pulsed in Shadowsight's paws. “I am still a medicine-cat apprentice!” Was he going to have to tell every cat he met now?

Sedgewhisker frowned. “But Bramblestar died because of you.”

Breezepelt nodded. “What cat is going to let you treat them after a leader died because of you?”

Shadowsight's mouth went dry. He felt light-headed when his anger turned to shame. He dropped his eyes and hurried away, leaving the empty leaf lying between the two WindClan warriors. Is that what every cat thinks? That Bramblestar died because of me? Shadowsight stopped suddenly before he reached the medicine den. Why bother? He did not belong there anymore. He did not belong anywhere.

If the real Bramblestar comes back, then they will know it was not my fault. And yet Shadowsight knew Bramblestar's ghost was missing. He had freed him from the vines that held him in the Dark Forest, but Bramblestar had not returned to the living forest. What if he was gone forever? Then I will know it was my fault.

Lost in his thoughts, Shadowsight watched Lightleap, his littermate, without really seeing her. She dragged an old nest out of the warriors' den. She dropped it beside the pile that Hollowspring and Gullswoop had made, and began to tear the nests apart. How could any cat forgive me? I have no future here. I would be better off as a loner.

Despair was like fog around him. It pressed on his chest until he could hardly breathe.

“Shadowsight?” Lightleap's mew sounded from far away, outside his cloud of sadness. “Are you okay?”

He watched her walk toward him. She felt very far away, even when she reached him and licked between his ears. “You look lost,” she mewed, leaning closer.

He blinked at her. The fog went away as he held on to her eyes.

“Do you need something to do?” she asked gently.

He nodded numbly.

She stepped back and shook bracken dust from her fur. “Blazefire and I were planning to hunt with Hollowspring and Gullswoop. Do you want to come with us?”

Shadowsight looked at the pile of half-opened nests. “Do you not have to finish cleaning the den?”

Lightleap sniffed. “We are not apprentices,” she mewed. When Shadowsight winced, she quickly added, “I just mean we can finish that later.” She looked over her shoulder to where Gullswoop was dragging fresh ferns into camp. “Can Shadowsight hunt with us?” she called.

Gullswoop dropped the ferns. Her eyes were bright. “He is welcome to try.”

“Try what?” Hollowspring padded into camp behind her, carrying more ferns.

“Shadowsight is coming hunting with us,” Gullswoop told him.

Was that anger shining in the black warrior's eyes?

Blazefire popped his head out of the den. His whiskers had little pieces of bracken on them. “Are we hunting now?”

“Yes!” Lightleap purred eagerly. “We have done enough cleaning for today.”

Shadowsight felt a little light of happiness, and his sadness began to melt. Thankfulness filled his chest. “Thanks, Lightleap.”

“We might be able to teach you a thing or two,” she mewed, leading the way out of camp.

Shadowsight followed. “You can teach me a lot. I do not know anything about hunting.”

Gullswoop walked beside him. “The first thing you have to learn is how to sit still.”

When they went into the forest, Blazefire and Hollowspring spread out. Shadowsight could see them tasting the air. Were they already looking for prey? He copied them and opened his mouth to let the air touch his tongue. He could smell the mint that grew near camp, and a patch of comfrey that grew between the bracken in this part of the forest. Another scent touched his tongue. Was that sage? He had not known it grew so close to camp.

“Rabbit!” Hollowspring's loud cry made him jump. Shadowsight's heart jumped too as the black warrior ran away fast, breaking through a wall of ferns. Blazefire ran after him, turning to one side.

“Will they not scare it with all that noise?” Shadowsight whispered to Lightleap.

“It had already seen us.” His littermate's eyes followed a gray blur far ahead between the trees. A rabbit was running fast toward a thick strip of brambles. Her fur stood up with excitement, and she did not take her eyes off it as she explained, “It was running away. Watch what Blazefire does.” She nodded toward the white-and-ginger tom. It was hard to believe Blazefire had been born in a Twolegplace. He was running over the forest floor like a diving hawk. He took the cleanest way between the trees and went around a ridge, while Hollowspring jumped over it. The rabbit was a few tail-lengths ahead of him.

“Why does not Hollowspring grab it?” Shadowsight asked. Surely, if the black warrior jumped now, he could catch the rabbit.

“Wait.” Lightleap was as still as stone as she watched her denmates.

Blazefire had changed his way now. He ran faster and turned around to block the rabbit's path. If it reached the brambles, Shadowsight guessed, they would lose it between the branches and thorns. As soon as Blazefire came where Hollowspring could see him, Hollowspring jumped. The rabbit turned sharply, changing its way, but Blazefire had come close. Quickly, he blocked the rabbit's path and jumped at it before it could reach the thorns.

Hollowspring slid to a stop. His tail moved fast with joy as Blazefire caught the rabbit with his teeth and lifted it by the scruff.

Lightleap purred. “Hollowspring could not be sure of catching it,” she explained to Shadowsight now that the hunt was over. “But he knew Blazefire would cut it off. A sure catch is better than a fast catch.”

Shadowsight blinked at his sister. He had not known she knew so much about hunting.

“Come on,” she mewed, walking away happily. “Let's go help bury it. We can pick it up on the way home.”

Shadowsight stayed back while Lightleap scooped out soft earth between the roots of a pine. Blazefire laid the rabbit in the hole and covered it with loose dirt and leaves. Gullswoop walked around the tree, her white tail moving with clear joy. The whole patrol seemed happy. Shadowsight had not known how much fun it was to be a warrior. He was used to working alone, gathering herbs or taking care of sick Clanmates. His life as a medicine cat suddenly seemed lonely compared to this. Especially now, when he was allowed to treat only one patient.

“Will you teach me how to smell prey?” he whispered to Lightleap as she began to lead the patrol through the forest once more. “All I can pick out is herbs.”

She purred. “I had not thought about that.” She stopped and tasted the air. “Can you smell what I smell?”

Shadowsight stopped beside her and opened his mouth.

Gullswoop, Blazefire, and Hollowspring stopped behind them.

“What are we looking for?” Gullswoop whispered.

“We are smelling for prey,” Lightleap told him.

Hollowspring sniffed. “I can smell a—”

“Quiet!” Lightleap cut him off. “Shadowsight needs practice.”

Shadowsight breathed deeper, trying not to notice the dock scent that hung heavy in the air here. He tasted a musky scent under it — a familiar musky scent. He concentrated, trying to remember which piece of prey from the pile of fresh prey it smelled like. Excitement ran through his paws as he remembered. “Mouse?”

Lightleap flicked her tail happily. “Well done.” She nodded toward a beech tree that had made a space for itself among the pines. “You often find mice around beech trees,” she told him. “They eat the nuts, so it is a good place to hunt in leaf-bare, when berries are scarce.”

“Look,” Hollowspring hissed. The black warrior had dropped his tail. He was staring at a blueberry patch a few tree-lengths away. Four sparrows were hopping among the leaves, pecking at the fruit that was beginning to ripen there.

Lightleap nodded, and the patrol moved quietly between the trees. Gullswoop and Blazefire moved in a wide circle around the sparrows, clearly planning to come to them from the far side of the blueberry patch. Shadowsight stayed close to Lightleap, hardly breathing. He did not want to scare the birds away.

When they came near, Lightleap nodded. “Stay back,” she told him. Her order was barely a whisper.

Shadowsight waited as the others closed in on the birds that did not see them. His heart was beating hard. How would they catch the sparrows before they flew away? The patrol moved closer quietly until they were only a few tail-lengths from their prey. A few more paw steps and they would be close enough to jump. Shadowsight watched and could not look away, wishing with all his heart that they would catch the birds.

Suddenly, a buzz sounded in his ear. His heart seemed to burst as a bee touched his ear fur. Its hum was so loud that he hardly heard his own cry of shock. He jumped backward, his fur standing up with panic. Crying with alarm, the sparrows flew up into the trees, their feathers ruffled with surprise.

Hollowspring turned to him and glared through the trees. “You mouse-brain!”

“I am sorry.” Shadowsight blinked at the black tom, confused and embarrassed. He looked over his shoulder, hoping the bee was gone. There was no sign of it.

“It is okay.” Lightleap went back to Shadowsight. Her mew was calm. “We will find some more.”

Hollowspring grunted, following her with Gullswoop and Blazefire. “What scared you?”

Shadowsight looked at the ground. “A bee buzzed in my ear.”

Blazefire's whiskers twitched. “A bee?” Shadowsight could tell he was trying to swallow back a purr.

“It was a big bee,” Shadowsight mewed defensively.

Gullswoop's eyes shone with amusement. “Bees are a warrior's greatest enemy.”

“More dangerous than foxes,” Blazefire teased.

Shadowsight looked at them sadly. At least they thought it was funny, unlike Hollowspring, who was still glaring at him.

“I guess it is okay for some cats to ruin a hunt,” the tom muttered with hurt. “I guess some cats can do whatever they like.”

Lightleap went stiff. “What do you mean by that?”

“Nothing.” Hollowspring looked away, angry.

Lightleap glared at him. “Say it!”

Hollowspring hesitated, then turned his eyes back to the brown she-cat. “I am not sure it is good to speak badly about one of Tigerstar's kin.”

Lightleap stared at him as if she did not know what to say. Gullswoop and Blazefire moved awkwardly.

Hollowspring turned away. “Come on,” he growled. “Let's catch something. The Clan will be hungry if we do not.”

Shadowsight's paws felt stuck to the ground. Is every cat in ShadowClan angry at me? Sadness crept under his fur like cold water. He had thought his Clanmates had not blamed him for Bramblestar's death because they liked and respected him enough to forgive him. But perhaps they had not forgiven him. They could be keeping quiet for fear of making Tigerstar angry. Shadowsight suddenly felt weak with dread. Had his Clanmates all turned against him without him knowing?
